What is FTP?

FTP (Functional Threshold Power) is the highest average power, in watts, you can hold for roughly an hour. It's the single most useful number in structured cycling training because nearly every workout target — endurance, tempo, sweet spot, threshold, VO2 max — is set as a percentage of it. Raise your FTP and every zone moves up with it.

What is watts-per-kilogram (W/kg), and why does it matter more than raw watts?

Two riders can both push 280 watts, but if one weighs 90 kg and the other 65 kg, they are not the same climber. W/kg — your FTP divided by your body weight — is what determines how fast you go uphill and how you stack up against other riders. On flat roads raw watts and aerodynamics matter more; the moment the road tilts up, W/kg is king.

To improve your W/kg you can raise the top (train to lift your FTP) or lower the bottom (lose non-functional weight) — most riders make the fastest gains by doing a bit of both, without ever under-fueling.

How to estimate your FTP without a lab

  • 20-minute test: Warm up, then ride as hard as you can sustain for 20 minutes. FTP ≈ your average power × 0.95. The most popular field test.
  • 8-minute test: Two 8-minute all-out efforts; take the best average × 0.90. Shorter and less painful, slightly less precise.
  • Ramp test: Power steps up every minute until you can't hold on. FTP ≈ 75% of the average power of your best full minute. Quick and repeatable indoors.
  • 60-minute effort: If you have a genuine ~1-hour max effort (like a hard time trial), that average is your FTP.

Whichever you use, test the same way each time so your numbers are comparable. Re-test every 4–6 weeks to track progress.

Rider categories by W/kg

The ranges in the calculator are based on the widely used Coggan power-profile framework, which maps threshold W/kg to broad ability levels from novice to world-class. They're a helpful gut-check, not a rulebook — real category racing depends on your local federation and results.

Is a good FTP the same for everyone?
No — that's exactly why W/kg exists. A "good" FTP for a 60 kg rider is very different from a 90 kg rider. Compare W/kg, and mostly compare yourself to your own past numbers.
How often should I retest?
Every 4–6 weeks during a training block. Testing too often just measures your fatigue, not your fitness.
Should I use my current weight or racing weight?
Use today's real weight for an honest W/kg. Chasing a lower number by under-eating tanks your power and your health — the goal is a higher ratio while fully fueled.
