TSS, CTL, ATL & TSB: Training Load, Explained

Fitness, Fatigue and Form — the three numbers behind every training chart, in plain English.

Open any training platform and you'll see a chart with three lines and a fistful of acronyms. They look intimidating, but the idea underneath is simple and genuinely useful: measure how much training you're doing, track how it builds fitness and fatigue over time, and use the gap between them to show up fresh on race day. Here's what each term means.

TSS — Training Stress Score

A single number for how hard one ride was, combining intensity and duration. An hour right at your FTP equals 100 TSS by definition. A long easy ride and a short brutal one can score the same — that's the point: it's your body's total "training cost" for the ride. Everything below is built from TSS.

CTL — Chronic Training Load = "Fitness"

A rolling ~42-day average of your daily TSS. Because it's a long average, it moves slowly — it represents the deep fitness you've accumulated over weeks and months. CTL only rises when you consistently do more than your recent norm. There are no shortcuts: fitness is built brick by brick.

ATL — Acute Training Load = "Fatigue"

A rolling ~7-day average of your daily TSS. Because it's short, it reacts fast — a few hard days spike it, a few rest days drop it. ATL is your current tiredness.

TSB — Training Stress Balance = "Form"

Simply CTL minus ATL — fitness minus fatigue. This is the money number:

  • Negative TSB — you're carrying fatigue. Normal and necessary during hard training blocks; that's where fitness is built.
  • Around zero — balanced.
  • Positive TSB — you're fresh. This is what you want on event day: fitness banked, fatigue shed.
The whole game: build CTL over months (get fit), then in the final 1–2 weeks let ATL fall while CTL stays high, pushing TSB positive. That's a taper — and it's why you can feel slow all season and then fly on race day.

How to actually use these

  • Grow CTL gradually. Ramping fitness too fast is the fastest route to injury or burnout — a rise of a few CTL points per week is plenty for most riders.
  • Expect negative TSB in training. Feeling a bit tired during a block is the plan working, not a problem.
  • Time your freshness. Aim to bring TSB positive for your key event, not for every weekend.
  • Don't chase the numbers for their own sake. They're a model, not the truth. How you actually feel — sleep, motivation, leg quality — always overrides the chart.

The one-sentence version

TSS = how hard today was. CTL = fitness (slow). ATL = fatigue (fast). TSB = form (fitness minus fatigue). Build the first, manage the last, and you'll show up strong when it counts.
