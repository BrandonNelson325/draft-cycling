Sweet Spot vs. Threshold: Which Should You Ride?

Two of the most effective — and most confused — training intensities. Here's what each does and when to use it.

Sweet spot and threshold are the two workhorses of FTP-building. They sit right next to each other on the power scale, they feel similar, and riders constantly mix them up. But they do slightly different jobs, and knowing which to reach for is the difference between steady progress and burning out. Both are defined as a percentage of your FTP, so you'll want a current number before you start.

Zone% of FTPFeels like
Sweet spot88–94%Hard but controlled — you could speak a short sentence
Threshold95–105%Right at your redline — talking is off the table

What sweet spot does

Sweet spot is the highest intensity you can do a lot of. It drives nearly the same aerobic adaptations as threshold work, but because it's a notch easier, you recover from it faster and can pack more of it into a week. That efficiency is why it has become the go-to for busy amateurs: maximum fitness return per hour of training, and per unit of suffering.

Typical sessions: 3 × 12 min, 2 × 20 min, or 4 × 10 min at 88–94% FTP, with short recoveries.

What threshold does

Threshold work trains your body to sustain power right at your ceiling and to clear lactate at that intensity. It's a more specific, more demanding stimulus — closer to race pace for time trials and hard climbs — but it costs more in fatigue, so you can do less of it.

Typical sessions: 3 × 10 min or 2 × 15 min at 95–105% FTP, with full recoveries.

The simple rule of thumb

Use sweet spot to build volume of quality work — especially in base and when time is tight. Use threshold to sharpen as an event approaches, or when you've built a base and want a stronger, more specific stimulus. Most riders should do more sweet spot than threshold.

Which should you ride?

  • Time-crunched (4–7 hrs/week): Lean heavily on sweet spot. It gives you the most fitness for your limited hours.
  • Building base, months from your event: Sweet spot, with easy endurance around it.
  • 6–8 weeks out from a goal event: Shift some sweet spot toward threshold to get specific.
  • Feeling flat or over-tired: Neither — you need easy rides and recovery, not more intensity.
Sweet spot and threshold are tools, not a religion. The best block rotates through both, sitting on a large base of easy Zone 2 riding — roughly 80% easy, 20% hard overall.

The bottom line

If you remember one thing: sweet spot builds the engine efficiently, threshold sharpens it specifically. Start most FTP-building blocks with sweet spot, layer in threshold as your event nears, and keep the easy days genuinely easy. Get that balance right and your FTP climbs — steadily, and without the burnout that comes from grinding every ride into the red.
