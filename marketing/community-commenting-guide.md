# Draft Cycling — Genuine Commenting Playbook

*How to show up in cycling communities as a helpful, recognizable person — no links, no spam, no getting banned — so that Draft becomes the name people already trust when they go looking for a training app.*

This is the "comment as a casual user" plan you asked for, done right. The whole thing rests on one idea:

> **You're not here to advertise. You're here to be the most helpful rider in the thread.** Reputation is the asset. Signups are a lagging side effect of it.

---

## The three rules (break these and it backfires)

1. **90/10 — and honestly closer to 97/3.** At least 9 out of 10 comments mention Draft *zero times*. Pure help. You're building a handle people recognize, not a billboard.
2. **Be yourself, disclose when relevant.** Never pose as an unaffiliated happy customer who "found this app." People read comment histories; getting caught astroturfing your own product is the one mistake a small brand doesn't recover from. When Draft is genuinely the answer, say *"I actually build a coaching app, so grain of salt, but…"* Founders who disclose get **more** trust, not less.
3. **No links in comments, almost ever.** Links trip spam filters and read as promo. Help without one. If someone explicitly asks "what do you use / where is it," *then* you can name it — once, plainly, with your disclosure.

## How to find the right threads (10 min/day)

**Reddit:** sort r/Velo and r/cycling by New once a day. Look for questions you can genuinely answer:
- "How do I train for [event]?" / "signed up for a century/gran fondo, where do I start?"
- "Is my FTP any good?" / "how do I raise my FTP?"
- "How do I fit training around a busy schedule?"
- "Sweet spot vs threshold?" / "what are power zones?"
- "Recovering from illness / time off — how do I get back?"
- "What training app should I use?" ← the rare one where you *may* mention Draft, disclosed.

**YouTube:** comment within the first hour on new uploads from the channels above — early comments get seen. Respond to the *content*, add value for other viewers.

**Facebook:** join 5–10 groups (your local clubs + 2–3 event-specific training groups + an indoor/Zwift group). Turn on notifications. Answer questions like a knowledgeable clubmate.

## Ready-to-adapt replies (no links, pure value)

*Adapt these to the specific thread — never paste verbatim, never the same wording twice. They mention Draft **not at all**. That's the point.*

**"How do I raise my FTP?"**
> The thing that helped me most was making my easy rides genuinely easy so my hard days could be actually hard. Most people ride everything at "medium," which is too hard to build a base and too easy to drive real gains. A simple week: one sweet-spot session (like 3×12 min at ~90% FTP), one threshold or VO2 session, one long easy ride, rest of the week easy or off. Progress it a little each week for 3 weeks, then take an easy week. Boring, but it works.

**"Signed up for a [century / gran fondo], how do I train?"**
> Congrats — that's a great goal. Two things matter most: total time in the saddle (your longest ride should build toward ~2/3 the event distance) and some structured intensity so the climbs don't wreck you. If you know the route, train for its specific demands — a flat century and a climbing one are totally different animals. Work backwards from the date: base first, then event-specific stuff in the last 6–8 weeks, then taper the final week. Happy to sketch a rough week if you share your schedule.

**"Is my FTP good?" / "I'm X watts at Y kg"**
> FTP on its own doesn't tell you much — it's watts-per-kg that matters once the road goes up. Divide your FTP by your weight in kg. Anything around 3 is a solid recreational rider, ~3.5–4 is strong, 4+ is getting genuinely quick. But honestly the most useful comparison is you vs. your own number a few months ago. Are you trending up?

**"How do I fit training around a crazy schedule?"**
> Quality over quantity, hard. 4–5 focused hours beats 8 junk-mile hours. Two short intense sessions midweek (sweet spot is your best friend when time-crunched — most fitness per minute) and one longer ride on your least-busy day. And build the plan to *flex* — if you miss a session, don't try to cram it back in, just move on. Consistency over months beats any perfect week.

**"Coming back after illness / time off"**
> Go slower than your ego wants. Rule of thumb: easy spinning for a few days after symptoms fully clear, then rebuild intensity over 1–2 weeks rather than jumping back to where you left off. Your fitness comes back faster than it built the first time, so patience early actually gets you back quicker overall.

**YouTube comment on a training-science video**
> Great breakdown. The point about polarizing intensity is the one I wish I'd understood years earlier — I spent ages in the grey zone wondering why I wasn't improving. Would love a follow-up on how you'd adjust this for someone who only has ~5 hours a week.

## The rare "what do you use?" moment (the disclosed 3%)

Only when someone **directly asks** what app/tool to use, or explicitly asks what you use:

> Full disclosure so I'm not being sneaky — I actually build a coaching app (Draft), so take it with a grain of salt. But for what you're describing I'd genuinely look at any structured-training tool that adapts when you miss sessions and lets you plan around your actual event. If you want, I can explain what to look for regardless of which one you pick.

Notice: disclosed, no link, and still leads with *neutral* advice. That's the version that converts *and* keeps your reputation clean. If they want the link, they'll ask — then give it once.

---

## Cadence (sustainable beats heroic)

- **15–20 min/day**, 5 days/week. Two or three genuinely helpful comments a day.
- Track nothing obsessively, but notice which of your comments get upvotes/replies — that tells you what resonates.
- Over 4–8 weeks you become a recognized name in 2–3 communities. *That's* when "what app should I use?" threads start tagging you unprompted. That's the whole payoff.

---

## Hard "don'ts"

- ❌ Don't post the same comment in multiple places (pattern spam — filters and mods catch it).
- ❌ Don't drop links. Ever, in normal comments.
- ❌ Don't pretend to be a random satisfied user. This is the brand-killer.
- ❌ Don't argue or get defensive if someone criticizes AI coaching. Agree with the valid parts, stay gracious.
- ❌ Don't DM people your product unsolicited.
- ❌ Don't upvote-ring or use alt accounts. One real identity.

**The mindset:** if you deleted the app tomorrow, these comments should still have been worth posting. Help that would stand on its own is the only kind that builds a brand.
